# Why now

Connect a current change to a known problem. A signal is useful only when it changes the likely cost of waiting or the value of acting.

## Steps

1. Define the target company or segment, problem, and time window.
2. Search for relevant changes such as hiring, funding, expansion, regulation, product launches, leadership changes, or public incidents.
3. Capture the source and the direct fact. State the bridge from the fact to urgency as an interpretation.
4. Rank signals by relevance, recency, specificity, and confidence. Separate a trigger from a buying decision.
5. Draft one timely message and one human validation question.

Read `../../references/evidence-policy.md`, `../../references/source-adapters.md`, and `../../references/artifact-contract.md`.

## Completion

The output states what changed, why it may matter, what it does not prove, and the next check before outreach.
